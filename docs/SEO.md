## 1. SEO basics: one-time setup

Do these in order once this branch is merged and deployed.

1. **Google Search Console.** Go to <https://search.google.com/search-console>, add a **URL-prefix** property for `https://geminii.browning.edu/`, and choose the **HTML tag** method. Copy only the `content` value. A Domain property would need DNS access to browning.edu.
2. **Bing Webmaster Tools.** Go to <https://www.bing.com/webmasters> and use **Import from Google Search Console**, which needs no tag. If you add the site manually instead, copy the `msvalidate.01` content value.
3. **Google Analytics 4.** Go to <https://analytics.google.com>, create a property and a **Web** data stream for `https://geminii.browning.edu`, and copy the Measurement ID (`G-…`).
4. **Set the repository variables.** In GitHub, open **Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions → Variables → New repository variable**:
   - `PUBLIC_GOOGLE_SITE_VERIFICATION`: the GSC token
   - `PUBLIC_BING_SITE_VERIFICATION`: the Bing token (optional if you imported from GSC)
   - `PUBLIC_GA_MEASUREMENT_ID`: the `G-…` ID

   Then re-run **Actions → Deploy to GitHub Pages → Run workflow**. Each tag appears only while its variable is set.
5. Click **Verify** in GSC (and Bing).
6. **Submit the sitemap.** In GSC go to **Sitemaps** and in Bing to **Sitemaps → Submit**, then enter `https://geminii.browning.edu/sitemap-index.xml`.
7. **Request indexing.** In GSC, run **URL Inspection** on `/`, `/about/`, `/outreach/` and `/giving/` and click **Request indexing** for each.
8. **Manual actions.** In GSC open **Security & Manual Actions → Manual actions**. It should say "No issues detected". A new site has none; re-check it monthly along with **Pages** (indexing) and **Core Web Vitals**.
9. **Confirm indexing.** About a week later, search Google and Bing for `site:geminii.browning.edu`. All four pages should appear. If any is missing, URL Inspection gives the reason.

Already automated:

- `/robots.txt` allows all crawlers and points to the sitemap (`src/pages/robots.txt.ts`).
- `/sitemap-index.xml` is generated by `@astrojs/sitemap` and leaves out the 404 page.
- After every deploy, the `indexnow` job in `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` sends every sitemap URL to IndexNow. That covers Bing, Yandex, Seznam, Naver and Yep. The key file is `public/941bb3b2062ec549708924f168e50f9f.txt`; don't delete it.
- Google dropped its sitemap "ping" endpoint in 2023. For Google, the sitemap submitted in GSC is the mechanism.

## 2. Keyword research

No paid keyword tool (Ahrefs, Semrush) was available, so search volume and difficulty below are estimated from the live results pages (SERPs), not measured. Once GSC has a few weeks of data, check **Performance → Queries** for real impressions.

### The core problem: the name

"Geminii" is one letter away from **Gemini Robotics**, Google DeepMind's robotics AI. That model has a Wikipedia page, DeepMind product pages and wide press coverage, so a search for "geminii robotics" is likely to be autocorrected or answered with Google's product. A search for "Geminii Robotics" in October 2026 returned only DeepMind and the GitHub repo. The site itself was not indexed yet.

Strategy: always pair the name with qualifiers Google can't confuse, such as **FTC**, **38977**, **Browning** and **NYC**. Answer the mix-up directly on the page; the About page FAQ now has "Is Geminii Robotics related to Google's Gemini Robotics?". Titles and descriptions already follow this pattern.

### Page intent of ranked results

- **Brand and team-number queries** surface the team's own site, plus FTC Events, FTCScout and Instagram. Searchers want "who is this team, how do I contact or follow them". The home page and the site-wide `SportsTeam` schema serve that.
- **"Browning School robotics"** results are the Ultro homepage and Browning news posts: informational and navigational. A visible mention and link on ultro.browning.edu and browning.edu is the realistic path.
- **"Donate to an FTC team"** results are fundraising pages (FIRST, booster clubs, Hack Club Bank). They are transactional, with clear steps and trust signals. The Giving page already has numbered steps, Browning as processor, and student-run finances.
- **"Gemini robotics"** results are about AI models, a different intent entirely. We only need to disambiguate, not compete.

### Questions people ask (answered in the About page FAQ)

1. What is Geminii Robotics?
2. Why is it spelled "Geminii"?
3. Is Geminii Robotics related to Google's Gemini Robotics?
4. How is Geminii related to Ultro Robotics?
5. What is FIRST Tech Challenge?
6. How can I support or sponsor the team?
7. How do I contact the team?

These also ship as `FAQPage` structured data. Google now shows FAQ rich results mainly for government and health sites, but the markup still helps other engines and AI answers pick up the text. Candidates for later, once the facts exist: "How do I join Geminii?", "When and where does Geminii compete?", "What robot did Geminii build this season?".

## 5. Ideas for growing content (highest value first)

1. **A season or robot page** (`/robots/` or `/season/2026-27/`): robot name, photos, events and awards. This gives Google new, linkable content each year and matches how Ultro's site is set up.
2. **Real photos** of the team, robot and outreach, with descriptive alt text. Image search is a real source of traffic for team sites.
3. **An outreach story** after each Carter Burden visit or Intro to Engineering session, which gives the local news and school paper something to link to.

### Unlinked mentions

As of this audit, the web has no mentions of "Geminii Robotics" or "38977" outside the GitHub repo. Every mention from now on should include a link. Once the site has data, check GSC's **Links** report, set a Google Alert for `"Geminii Robotics"`, and email any page that mentions the team without a link.

### New link opportunities

- Event recaps: NYC FIRST qualifiers and scrimmages (including Browning's own citywide scrimmage) often publish team lists with links.
- Sponsor and partner pages: any company that sponsors parts or tools usually lists sponsors with links.
- Local STEM directories and NYC student-opportunity lists.
- Robotics community posts (Chief Delphi, r/FTC) that share CAD, code or season write-ups, linked back to a season page on the site.

### Google Business Profile (GMB)

**Recommendation: don't create a separate profile.** Google Business Profiles are for organisations that serve customers in person at a location. A school team inside The Browning School, at the school's address, would duplicate Browning's existing listing, and Google may suspend it. Instead:

- Ask Browning Communications to post about Geminii on the **school's** Business Profile (Updates) with a link to the site.
- The site's `SportsTeam` schema already ties the team to the school's address, which is how Google connects the two.

If the team ever has its own public venue or events address, reconsider then.
